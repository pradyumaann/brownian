# Brownian: simulated user reactions, 18 Nov 2026

**Read this first.** These are 10 SIMULATED personas played by an AI (one sub-agent each), reading a frozen snapshot of the app (the mock Fri 13 Nov 2026 brief). They are not real users. Treat everything here as hypotheses to test with real people. Never quote a persona as user evidence, a testimonial, a usage number or a willingness-to-pay figure (product rule 4: never fabricate users, testimonials, usage numbers or pricing).

Snapshot: static export of Markets (Overnight), Portfolio Lens, Wander, Flows, India, Global, Macro, Earnings, Developing, Learn; screenshots at 1280 and 400px, night and paper.

## 1. Reliability caveats (affects how far to trust this)

- **Screenshots were seen heavily downscaled.** Phone shots (400 x about 8,600 px) reached the personas at about 120 px wide. Layout, section order, colour and length were judged; **type size, tap targets and legibility were NOT verified by anyone.** Phone readability scores (mean 5.0) and the retiree's large-text verdict are therefore low-confidence. Re-check with real device screenshots.
- **Coverage was uneven.** Everyone read the front page. Wander was read fully only by 3 personas (partly by 5); Portfolio Lens by 7; Jargon Buster by 4 (beginner, SIP, Hindi-first, retiree partly). India, Global, Earnings and Developing were opened by 1 to 3 personas each. Nobody except the NRI read Global Cues in full. Conclusions about those views are thin.
- **Shared-text artefacts.** All personas read the same text, so the same phrases recur (for example "Expect MCX bullion to open lower", "biggest single-stock risk", "Herfindahl 0.25"). Repetition shows those phrases are salient, not that 10 independent people noticed them.
- **Scores cluster.** Most scores fall between 3 and 9 and the non-expert personas gave near-identical profiles (overload 9, clarity 3, emotion 3). Real people differ more. Persona-specific answers that looked most generic: the three non-investor-ish phone personas (beginner, SIP, Hindi-first) overlapped heavily. The most distinctive and credible: the CA skeptic (specific statistical and sourcing critique), the F&O trader (specific missing data) and the NRI (timezone insight).
- **Pay figures are not reliable.** AI personas state conditional prices easily. Use only as a rough signal of which feature each segment names.
- **Possible data oddities raised by personas, not verified by me:** "Put/call ratio removed on 15 Nov 2026" appearing on a 13 Nov brief; GIFT Nifty stamped 02:44 IST on a 07:52 brief; USD/INR 94.04 (brief) vs 93.70 (Macro); "+11.3% over 20 sessions" vs "+13.13% 21 sessions" for Nifty. The pages explain some of these, but they read as inconsistency. Worth a check by whoever owns the data.

## 3. Segment insights

**Works for (partly):**
- **DIY long-term investor (P3, primary user):** lead paragraph and four tiles give the open in about 30 seconds (clarity 8); likes the sourcing, the Flow Map "everyone else net sold" framing and the Wander honesty. But return is only 6: the example weights and 10 asset-type buckets mean his 18 stocks vanish into "Bank, NBFC & insurance stocks", and "Your day" is "a made-up number". He would pay 299 only if real holdings drive the page.
- **NRI (P6):** rupee tile, Global Cues, IST event times and FPI flows hit his needs (clarity 8, trust 8). Fails on timing (07:52 IST brief lands 06:22 Gulf time), NRE/NRO, US holdings and USD-based returns.
- **Skeptic (P7):** respects the methodology disclosure and candid statistics, wants it as a citeable data layer, but sees an advice-shaped surface and statistical weaknesses (equal weights, three components on the Nifty itself, overlapping windows, percentile saturation).

**Loses:**
- **Beginner (P1), SIP investor (P2), Hindi-first (P10), retiree (P5):** all hit the same wall. The first screen is GIFT Nifty, FII, 10-year yield, Brent; nothing tells them "your SIP / FD needs nothing today". Emotional effect is 2 to 3: red "-0.42%" on an example mix that is not theirs, "Greed" beside "macro pressure high", "Overnight news turned against India". They would close it, and two said they would call a distributor or open Groww or YouTube instead. This is the main safety finding: an alarm without a settling sentence.
- **Gen-Z (P8):** likes the Wander dial as a vibe and the Earnings scorecard, but the front page is about 13 screens; relevance 2 (no small caps, IPOs or crypto-adjacent). Would screenshot the dial for Telegram, not use the app.
- **F&O trader (P4):** not the target, and confirmed: no levels, OI, PCR, max pain or FII futures long/short. Relevance 3. Expected outcome; do not chase.
- **HNI (P9):** wants "so what for me" in 3 lines; praises "Higher odds of another RBI rate hike" and the household-costs note (overseas tuition), but cannot get his PMS or business reflected.

**Interpretation:** the product's content is credible but its **default surface is written for someone who already owns the example mix and knows the vocabulary**. Personalisation and a plain top summary are the two gates between 4.5 mean return and a usable daily habit. Confidence: medium-high that overload and missing personalisation are real; medium that lay users would feel as alarmed as simulated; low on phone type size.

## 5. Risks: advice-like or alarming

**Reads as advice or forecast (one persona rated 7; nine rated 3).** Quoted by personas from the pages:
- "Expect MCX bullion to open lower": a prediction (flagged by P2, P4-adjacent, P5, P9, P10, P3).
- "If you hold a Nasdaq or US tech fund, this is the biggest single-stock risk": directed at a holder (P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7, P9, P10).
- "If you hold it as a long-term hedge, a dip changes the price, not the reason you hold it": behaviour nudge (P1, P2, P5, P9).
- "Sharp runs carry pullback risk"; "Treat as a one-day spike until it follows through" (Realty); "New FDs may pay more if the RBI keeps tightening"; "Short-duration funds are less affected than long-duration funds".
- **Labels:** Tailwind / Headwind / Mixed per holding type, "High impact", "Medium link 2 of 4", "Watch:" on every Developing item, quadrant "Fragile optimism". P7 says these directional per-holding labels plus "watch" lines are what a regulator could read as personalised research. She recommends legal review. (She cited no rule; neither do I.)
- "Sentiment is a contrarian signal" and the Baker-Wurgler line on speculative stocks were read by P2 and P8 as a nudge to sell when others are greedy, or against their small-cap plan.
- Disclaimer "For information only; not investment advice" is in the footer, grey and small, per P5 and P7.

**Likely to alarm:** big red "-0.42%" on an example; "gold -2.88% to a 13-month low"; "Greed" next to "macro pressure 79 High"; "Extreme" turbulence; "Overnight news turned against India"; "Indian inflation broke the RBI's limit"; "rate hike"; "Nvidia's CEO resigned" (mock data). Consequences stated by personas: P5 would phone her distributor about her debt funds; P10 would "go to bed worried"; P1 felt she should not start investing; P2 felt "I'm missing something". Counter-evidence: the calming lines worked ("Markets wander. You don't have to.", "A single day's move rarely changes a long-term plan", "Reading it rationally"). They are present but buried; the alarm comes first.

## 8. Validate with real people

**Questions and methods**
1. Can a first-time visitor say what happened in the market and whether their SIP/FD needs action, in 5 seconds? Five-second test on the top card, 5 users per segment (beginner, SIP, saver, DIY).
2. Does a neutral "Example" tag versus a red -0.42% change anxiety? A/B in a prototype, or short interviews showing both.
3. Would DIY investors enter real holdings (10 minutes) in exchange for a personalised top strip? Fake-door "Add my holdings / paste tickers" button with click and drop-off tracking; 5 interviews with direct-stock investors.
4. Do labels like Tailwind/Headwind/High impact read as advice to lay readers, and to a SEBI-literate lawyer? 5 interviews plus a legal read. This one could change product rules, so give it priority.
5. Do people understand the Wander dial (68/79)? Comprehension test: "what would you do if it said 68?" Check for misreadings as a buy/sell signal.
6. Is Hindi demand real? Fake-door language toggle and 5 interviews with Hindi-first investors (do not rely on the simulated demand).
7. Phone legibility and tap targets at large font, on real devices (not tested in this simulation).
8. Will anyone pay? Interviews on what they would pay for and what they pay today, never quoting the simulated numbers.

**Instrument once real users exist** (aggregate only, consistent with device-only storage of holdings):
- Activation: first visit that scrolls past the top card; Portfolio Lens weights set (yes/no, count of types set).
- Retention: day-1, day-7, day-30 return; time of day of visit versus 09:15 IST.
- Scroll depth per section and drop-off section; time to first interaction.
- Source-tag opens ([S#]/[W#]) and "The detail" opens; Jargon Buster opens and which terms; Term tooltip opens once added.
- Which nav tabs are used; theme (night/paper); phone versus desktop share; font-size or zoom setting; language toggle clicks.
- Top-card variant exposure and outcome; fake-door click rates.
